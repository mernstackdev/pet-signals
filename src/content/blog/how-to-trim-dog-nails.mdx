## The short answer

To trim your dog's nails safely, work in short sessions, take thin slivers rather than one big cut, and stop before you reach the quick — the pink, blood-filled core inside the nail. Sharp clippers, good lighting, and a treat after every nail make the job faster and far less stressful for both of you.

## What you need

You need one good tool and one backup. A pair of sharp scissor or guillotine clippers sized for your dog does the job; blunt blades crush the nail and make it split. Keep styptic powder on hand in case you catch the quick. A second person to hold a steady smear of peanut butter is optional but very effective on food-motivated dogs.

## Finding the quick (and how to avoid it)

The quick is the sensitive, blood-filled centre of the nail. On white or light nails it is easy to see as a pink core. On black nails it is invisible, so you trim by watching the cut surface: when a grey or pink oval appears in the middle of the sliced nail, you are close and should stop. When in doubt, cut less. A slightly long nail is better than a bleeding one.

## How often to trim

Most dogs need a trim every three to four weeks. If you can hear nails clicking on hard floors, they are already a little long. Nails that are left too long can slowly push the toes out of alignment and make walking uncomfortable.

## If your dog hates it

Do not force a full trim in one go. Break the job into micro-sessions: one paw today, the next paw tomorrow, with a treat after every single nail. Practise simply holding the clippers near a paw without cutting anything, then build up. For dogs who are genuinely fearful, a groomer or vet can trim them while you work on cooperative care at home.

## When to see a groomer or vet

See a professional if a nail is cracked, curled into the pad, or torn, or if you have cut the quick and the bleeding does not stop within a few minutes. Ingrown nails are painful and need a steady hand; leave those to your vet rather than risking a deeper cut at home.
